## Step 2 — Research Each Career Path

For each career, use O*NET, the BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, LinkedIn, and any other credible source to gather the following:

### Career 1

**Career Title:**
Offensive Cyber Operations Specialist

**Brief Description** *(what does this person do day to day?):*
An Offensive Cyber Operations Specialist Plans & supports cyber operations against adversary system & networks. Day-to-day work can typically include analyzing networks & threats, identifying vulnerabilities, conducting cyber operations, supporting mission planning, & working with other cyber teams to create effects in cyberspace while also protecting friendly systems. 

**Typical Education Required:**
Education rq. depend on the operation. Military Cyber Operations Specialist positions can begin w/ a high school diploma or GED followed by extensive military cyber training, while civilian offensive cyber positions commonly prefer or require a bachelor’s degree in CyberSecurity, Computer Science, Information Assurance, or a related technical field.

**Key Skills Required** *(list at least 6):*
1. Network security & computer networking 
2. Linux & Windows operating systems
3. Programming & Scripting
4. Vulnerability analysis 
5. Penetration testing 
6. Cyber threat analysis

**Key Knowledge Areas Required:**
Computer networking, operating systems, cybersecurity, vulnerability assessment, penetration testing, cyber threat intelligence, programming, and offensive and defensive cyber operations

**Median Annual Salary:**
Approximately $124,910 per year for Information Security Analysts, the closest BLS civilian occupational category.

**Job Growth Outlook** *(growing, stable, declining?):*
Growing — employment for Information Security Analysts is projected to grow 29% from 2024 to 2034.

**Typical Career Path** *(how do people enter and advance in this field?):*
A common civilian path is earning a bachelor's degree in cybersecurity, computer science, or a related field, developing networking and system administration skills, earning cybersecurity certifications, and gaining experience through security operations, penetration testing, or threat analysis. Another path is entering military cyber operations, completing specialized cyber training, gaining operational experience and a security clearance, and advancing into more specialized or leadership cyber roles.

**3 specific courses or experiences that would build skills for this career:**
1. Network Security and Computer Networking courses
2. Ethical Hacking / Penetration Testing courses and hands-on cyber labs
3. Cybersecurity internship, Capture the Flag competitions, or home-lab experience

### Career 2

**Career Title:**
Electronic Warfare Enginee

**Brief Description** *(what does this person do day to day?):*
An Electronic Warfare Engineer develops, tests, and improves systems that operate within the electromagnetic spectrum. Day-to-day work can include analyzing radar and radio-frequency signals, testing electronic warfare systems, developing countermeasures, integrating hardware and software, troubleshooting systems, and supporting technologies used to detect, disrupt, or protect against adversary electronic systems.

**Typical Education Required:**
A bachelor's degree in Electrical Engineering, Computer Engineering, Computer Science, Cybersecurity, or a related technical field is typically required. Advanced or specialized positions may also require experience with RF systems, signal processing, embedded systems, cybersecurity, or defense technology.

**Key Skills Required** *(list at least 6):*
1. Radio Frequency (RF) systems
2. Signal processing
3. Computer networking
4. Programming and scripting
5. Cybersecurity and vulnerability analysis
6. Electronic warfare systems

**Key Knowledge Areas Required:**
Radio-frequency communications, radar, electromagnetic spectrum operations, signal processing, antennas, computer networks, embedded systems, cybersecurity, electronic countermeasures, and defense systems integration.

**Median Annual Salary:**
Approximately $118,780 per year using Electrical and Electronics Engineers as the closest BLS occupational category.

**Job Growth Outlook** *(growing, stable, declining?):*
Growing — employment of electrical and electronics engineers is projected to grow about 7% from 2024 to 2034.

**Typical Career Path** *(how do people enter and advance in this field?):*
A common path is earning a bachelor's degree in electrical engineering, computer engineering, computer science, cybersecurity, or a related field. Entry-level engineers can gain experience with RF systems, radar, signal processing, embedded systems, and defense technology. With experience, they can advance into specialized electronic warfare, systems engineering, technical leadership, or defense research roles.

**3 specific courses or experiences that would build skills for this career:**
1. Electrical Engineering or RF Communications coursework
2. Cybersecurity, Networking, and Embedded Systems coursework
3. Internship or hands-on project involving drones, radio systems, sensors, or defense technology
